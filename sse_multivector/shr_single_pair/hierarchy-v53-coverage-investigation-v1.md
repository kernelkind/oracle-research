## Assumptions tested

### Reranking existing saved candidates will fix coverage

An exact maximum-coverage calculation selected at most six sets from 538 saved
root candidates. Their union contains 979 notes, but the optimum six-set coverage
is 670, equal to the saved root-stage result. Requiring preservation of all 670
root-stage notes gives the same optimum. Solver gap: zero.

This bounds only this recorded candidate pool. Candidates depend on the actual
construction history; this is not proof about all possible counterfactual runs.
It does show that reranking these candidates alone cannot repair the loss.

### More root anchors or another unchanged membership pass will fix it

Using the completed global untracked pool with each root's direct originals in
the native V5.3 numerical path proposes six additional notes. Replacing those
anchors with all recursive original supports proposes zero. Adding anchors is
not a monotone expansion of HDBSCAN membership.

Fitting HDBSCAN on root supports alone and using `approximate_predict` on omitted
entries proposes two notes with recursive supports. Both strengths are below
0.5. This does not provide a substantial replacement. Prediction strength is not
a probability of semantic containment.

### Existing graph membership supplies correct classifier labels

It does not. Two existing recursive memberships conflict with their fixed scope:

- `L06298`, **Money posting destination**, is supported under `C00154`, Monetary
  resources, despite defining a destination's name rather than money.
- `L00860`, **Musicians and people who know musicians**, is supported under
  `C00062`, Musical arts, despite also admitting acquaintances who need not make
  music or act in a musical role.

These are agent diagnoses from the saved meanings, not new human judgments.
They are training positives in the completed-graph classifier, so their later
acceptance is not an independent held-out error rate. It reveals incorrect input
labels instead. Conversely, a missing relationship to Food does not prove that an
entry supported by Life or Information lies outside Food.

### A high-purity numerical community supplies a safe semantic expansion

A graph of full-meaning lexical similarities produces 33 natural communities.
Requiring all existing support notes in a community to support the same root
still proposes BMI/Obesity and Hydrogen under Finance. Relaxing unanimity admits
more notes and more scope errors. This is not an adoption candidate.

Forced six-way partitions, ordinary linkage cuts, graph community methods and
block models also produced misleading mixtures. Mechanical coverage of 976 or
999 notes does not establish useful categories or valid membership.

### Two declared numerical probes

The member-affinity component of
[Tip-Adapter (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09519) motivates a positive weight
for each member: `K(q,x) = exp[-beta * (1 - cosine(q,x))]`. Mean affinity over all
175 Food members is the first score. Mean affinity within each original group
is retained separately. Using the mean avoids treating a larger group as
stronger merely because it has more entries.

The second score is `log(mean_food_affinity / mean_background_affinity)`.
[Bayesian Sets (2005)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2005/file/182e6c2d3d78eef40e5dac7da77a748f-Paper.pdf)
motivates comparing support from supplied examples with background support.
This is a kernel-density-ratio adaptation, not the paper's Bayesian model or
an estimated probability of containment. Neither full Tip-Adapter nor SetExpan
or EgoSet is implemented here. No synthetic-root vector contributes to a score.

The plan freezes beta 5.5 as the primary setting, borrowing a published moderate
Tip-Adapter setting without fitting SSE labels. Beta 1 and 20 are predeclared
sensitivity probes. No threshold, classifier or representation was fitted or
selected from the results. Title, full-meaning and first-sentence pooling use
the same complete `title + "\n\n" + meaning` context.

## Scaling group matches by internal member similarity

The user authorized testing whether a candidate's similarity to an original
group becomes more useful when compared with that group's own internal
similarity. This is fixed arithmetic over saved full-meaning vectors. No
labels, group membership, meanings, encoder, root definitions or hierarchy
edges change. There are no new encodings, LLM calls, training or paid resources.

### Frozen rule and comparison

Use the existing kernel `K(a,b) = exp(-5.5 * (1 - cosine(a,b)))`.
For a query and each original reference group:

1. Exclude all references from the query's source note.
2. Calculate the mean query-to-reference affinity.
3. Calculate the mean affinity between the remaining references, excluding
   every self-pair and every pair from the same source note.
4. Divide step 2 by step 3. The root's score is the largest such ratio among
   its eligible original groups.

The denominator is a reference similarity scale, not a probability or semantic
label. A group without two independent source notes would remain unavailable,
not receive an invented denominator. No such unavailable group occurs for
these fixed queries. Initially ungrouped references are not combined into a
fabricated group. They remain in the all-member-mean baseline, but not in
either best-original-group method.

Three scores are compared: the unchanged all-member mean, the raw best-group
mean on the same grouped references, and the adjusted best-group mean. Thus
adjustment can be separated from simply selecting a best group. The raw best
group's poor earlier result remains a control, not a discarded approach.
All old-panel baseline counts reproduce exactly before interpreting the new
score.

The frozen older panel has 142 saved root/note assessments; the later panel
has 86. A focused panel contains the preceding twenty cases plus all twelve
previously reviewed useful or qualified Tools cases below the publisher score.
Panels overlap and are not independent samples. All case meanings and winners
remain fixed; no other entry in a note is substituted to improve a method.
Qualified and unresolved assessments remain separate. Most assessments are
provisional agent reviews, not human semantic gold; prior human source-note
decisions and attribution are preserved.

## Title/meaning neighborhood fusion

The user authorized testing whether title and meaning neighbor relationships
can be combined more usefully than an arithmetic cosine blend. This bounded
probe reuses the original 137 Food and 67 Tools references, both saved vector
representations and the same 180 query entries. It makes no new encodings,
LLM calls, training runs, semantic reviews, hierarchy changes or UI updates.
Only reference grouping changes; candidate scoring stays in meaning space.

### Frozen calculation and controls

The experimental module is `sse_multivector/similarity_network_fusion.py`.
It follows the exchange and normalization equations of the authors'
[SNFtool implementation](https://github.com/maxconway/SNFtool/blob/master/R/SNF.R),
using neighbor count twenty, local scale multiplier `0.5` and twenty iterations.
Those settings were frozen before scoring and were not tuned on the judgments.
This is a **bounded sparse adaptation**, not an exact implementation of dense
published SNF:

1. Build separate title and meaning networks from angular distances, with
   locally scaled Gaussian affinities on mutual twenty-neighbor connections.
2. Normalize each network. Update the title network by passing the meaning
   network through title-neighbor transitions, and update meaning through title
   information simultaneously. Add identity mass and symmetrize each round.
3. Limit each state to twenty mutual non-self connections per entry. Prune the
   first intermediate product before the second multiplication, preventing
   unbounded dense diffusion. The final graph has the same degree limit.
4. Extract EOM groups using HDBSCAN on reciprocal nonzero edge affinities.
   Missing connections remain infinite distances, with no invented edge.
   Minimum cluster size is two; minimum samples is three including the entry.

Two matched controls use identical inputs and graph machinery: **Unfused** has
zero diffusion iterations; **Self diffusion** runs twenty iterations but each
representation exchanges only with itself. The latter distinguishes mixing
the representations from diffusion alone. All resulting group memberships,
noise entries, warnings and intermediate sparsity counts remain saved.

The existing native angular decoder does not accept an arbitrary fused network.
The numerical probe therefore uses scikit-learn's precomputed-distance HDBSCAN.
Angular-distance replay reproduces both saved title memberships exactly. Meaning
replays differ by three Food and four Tools entries: 66 versus 63 and 45 versus
41 assigned entries respectively. Both native and library-reference controls
are retained separately. This implementation difference is not attributed to
the representation or declared a semantic error. An amendment recording these
controls was frozen before query scoring; no clustering settings changed.
Unfused, self-diffusion and exchanged-fusion comparisons share the same decoder.

### Frozen query rule and controls

The experimental `sse_multivector/fixed_reference_query.py` computes support
from the two query vectors and the already-saved accepted connections. For each
group, let `t_i` be the outsider's title affinity to accepted entry `i`, `m_j`
its meaning affinity to accepted entry `j`, and `C_ij` their fixed connection.
Both affinities use the unchanged kernel `exp[-5.5 * (1 - cosine)]`.

The combined score is:

```
sqrt(sum(C_ij * t_i * m_j) / sum(C_ij))
```

Self-connections and connections between entries from the same source note are
excluded. All entries from the query's own source note are also excluded. This
lets title and meaning match different accepted entries when those entries have
a saved connection. No outside connection is written back. Every query is
independent of other queries, and swapping its two views preserves the score
because the accepted connections are symmetric.

Matched single-view controls substitute `t_i * t_j` or `m_i * m_j` into exactly
the same weighted calculation. A separate control combines both views at each
same reference entry, `sqrt(mean(t_i * m_i))`, without accepted connections.
It uses the same eligible groups. This is the stated square-root-of-mean-product
control, not a claim to have tested every arithmetic vector blend.

The primary comparison uses the largest raw group score. Formulas and controls
were frozen before query scores were calculated. No weights, thresholds or
labels are fitted. This custom query rule is not published SNF inference or a
Nyström implementation. The literature confirms that extending a fixed graph
to new inputs is a separate problem, but does not validate this proposed score:
[Bengio et al., 2003](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/hash/cf05968255451bdefe3c5bc64d550517-Abstract.html).

### Frozen graph and boundary calculation

The existing `similarity_network_fusion.local_graph` constructs mutual
20-neighbor connections with its existing local scale multiplier `0.5`.
Self edges and edges between entries from the same source note are then removed.
As in that existing constructor, same-note entries may consume places during
neighbor retrieval before those edges are removed. The full graph has 72,836
directed non-self edges, 144 connected components and 134 isolated entries.
Its largest component contains 6,784 entries. No dense all-pairs graph is stored.

For each accepted group independently:

1. Give its source notes equal starting mass, divided among their group entries.
2. Compute personalized PageRank with continuation `0.85`; divide by graph degree
   to order nearby entries. The numerical convergence tolerance is `1e-12`, not
   an admission score.
3. Keep all original group entries, then examine progressively larger prefixes
   of that order. Calculate **conductance**: connection weight crossing the set
   boundary divided by the smaller side's total incident connection weight.
4. Select the smallest minimum-conductance prefix among those containing at most
   half the graph's total incident weight. If that constrained minimum has no
   local valley, propose no additions. A seed-only result is also allowed.
5. Union the separate group proposals for each root. Roots can overlap; membership
   in another root never supplies a rejection label.

The preselected secondary control uses the **first local minimum** instead of
the lowest one. It is reported regardless of whether it looks better. Two
continuation controls (`0.5`, `0.95`) and two candidate-pool controls were also
frozen before results. No label selects these settings or boundaries.

This is a seed-preserving adaptation of the conductance sweep described in
[local graph-clustering research](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/jure/pubs/mappr-kdd17.pdf).
It does not inherit an unqualified semantic guarantee from that research. Its
boundary adapts to the graph, but graph construction and diffusion still have
parameters. Numerical clustering is not semantic model training.

## Approved return to fixed fusion references; adaptive-boundary proposal

The user explicitly decided: "we will stick w the fusion setup and evaluate each

The admission boundary remains unresolved. The following proposal was tested in
the next section; it is not an adopted admission algorithm:

1. Keep the existing fusion reference and direct mean-affinity query score fixed.
   For an entry `q` and group `G`, average the existing
   `exp[-5.5 * (1 - meaning_cosine(q, r))]` over available members `r` of `G`.
   Exclude every reference from `q`'s source note. Do not compare that score
   directly with a fused edge weight: those measurements have different units.
2. Calculate that same query score for accepted reference entries, using only
   the fixed accepted pool of the same root as context. The current pools used
   to build fusion contain 137 Food and 67 Tools entries; this introduces no
   previously unaccepted reference. Include the group's surrounding accepted
   entries, not only its own members. In particular, a two-entry group supplies
   only one cross-note pair similarity and cannot alone justify a curve bend.
   Other accepted topics are geometric context, not root-level negatives.
3. Form a reference-only support curve, ordering these accepted scores and
   giving each source note equal total weight. Investigate the original bend
   principle: normalize the axes, inspect the central 80%, and find the largest
   departure from the endpoint chord marking the end of the strongly supported
   neighborhood. This must specify and preserve the bend orientation; the old
   outsider-score bend and the hierarchy split-distance bend are not identical
   calculations. No outsider score enters this reference curve. A missing bend
   remains unresolved rather than triggering a hand-selected score or percentile.
4. Check whether the resulting boundary can recover the group's existing
   members with their entire source note excluded from both the reference and
   boundary calculation. A group whose meaning-support geometry cannot support
   such a boundary remains unavailable for automatic expansion; its accepted
   memberships are retained. This internal check is necessary, not semantic
   certification, and does not authorize altering groups or tuning parameters.
5. Evaluate a new outsider against the resulting group-specific boundaries.
   Several groups or roots may provide support; there is no exclusive winner
   or other-root rejection. Boundaries can differ between groups and reference
   versions, but the same query/reference version must produce the same result
   when other outsiders are added, removed, duplicated or reordered.

The distinction from earlier failed ending rules is concrete: those bent a
curve made from the outsiders being selected, or used parity with one reference
average. This proposal uses accepted-reference structure to derive a local
boundary and separately checks whether that structure can support it. It is
still possible for a reference-only bend to give an unsuitable boundary; that
must be measured rather than assumed away. A weak group must not acquire broad
admission simply because its reference scores are low.
