## Observed progression

V125's first four definitions require edible material in their opening clauses.
Risotto separately asks whether an ingredient must independently nourish and
includes salt, herbs and seasonings through their culinary role. Pie examines
whether mere edibility establishes the intended role. These are consequential
questions beyond broad-category-versus-example differences.

However, v125's pie repeat opens with material intended to be eaten or drunk for
nourishment. Edible ingredients appear only in a subsequent included subset.
The HR09 poisonous dish mistakenly intended as nourishment satisfies that broad
head. The older deliberately harmful poisoned-drink hypothesis does not supply
the same nourishment intention and must not be substituted for this stronger
witness. A qualified agent rejection preserves the repeat; the first draw is
not spliced into it.

All **six observed v126 definitions** preserve a physical qualifying condition
in the whole defining clause: edible material, or material suitable for eating
or drinking directly or after preparation. This excludes the explicitly
non-edible poison and the already poisonous intended-nourishment dish. Later
examples are not used to supply a missing global condition. Preparation-dependent
suitability is not arbitrary theoretical conversion of harmful material into
food, and does not establish that preparation occurred.

V126's first pie draw explicitly distinguishes edibility/intention from actual
eating, and states that intention alone does not make an inedible object food.
Its repeat retains global edibility and human nourishment/enjoyment conditions.
Brisket directly examines human versus animal scope and raw versus completed
preparation; it does not invent completed cooking or consumption. Named source
ingredients remain covered, including non-nourishing seasonings. No unsupported
particular fact or concrete loss of an original ingredient is established.

This is bounded improvement over the v125 pie recurrence, not a reliability
estimate or uniquely established causal attribution. No overall semantic pass
rate or convergence of the Food definitions is claimed.

## Purpose and ingredient qualifications remain

V126 does not impose universal nourishment or human-consumer gold. Its exact
purposes still vary across independent definitions:

- First risotto: nourishment or an ingredient role in preparing/flavoring
  dishes. Diagnostic beads can qualify on a broad preparation reading when
  mixed into porridge; the culinary contribution is not explicitly settled.
- Sourdough: nourishment or part of meals. Diagnostic beads may fit if the
  porridge is a meal, which the saved hypothesis does not explicitly establish.
- First and repeated pie: human nourishment or enjoyment. The specified
  clinical-only hypotheses fail those purposes.
- Brisket: human nourishment with suitability before or after preparation.
- Repeated risotto: edible material for direct human eating/drinking or an
  ingredient role. The edible clinical wafer fits its direct-consumption
  alternative; diagnostic drinks and medicinal oil remain conditional on their
  unstated edibility. Ceremonial swallowing must qualify as eating/drinking;
  that route cannot silently be assumed. This broad scope matches its first
  resolution, which never requires nourishment.

The repeated risotto's culinary-ingredient subset cannot narrow its unrestricted
direct-consumption alternative. Clinical admission therefore is not by itself
an established producer failure. Its relationship between broad preparation
and the more specific culinary-ingredient conditions remains qualified.

The next proposed diagnostic is to distinguish direct-consumption scope from
ingredient-role scope using the existing standalone wafer and tracer beads
mixed into porridge. Inspect what each final meaning admits and whether its
resolutions actually choose those boundaries. Do not edit merely to force
clinical-negative labels. Broader standalone/branch qualification is still
needed before recommending v126 for general hierarchy generation.
