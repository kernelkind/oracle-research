# CAP04 — word order and adjacency in category candidates

## Question and decision

CAP03 contains useful shared descriptions that its seed family cannot propose.
It also loses the attachment in “more complete disconnection” when the words
are pooled with “More coffee.” Before adding a parser or phrase-quality model,
test what keeping word order and adjacency actually buys on the frozen inputs.

This is a candidate-generation diagnostic, not a new hierarchy. A candidate is
a repeated pair of words, not a certified subject or a complete descriptor.
Membership means that this exact pattern occurs in a saved subject; it does not
certify semantic membership. All saved subjects remain linked to their original
masks and full notes. No generated subject is substituted for source evidence.

## Controlled experiment

Use all 945 CAP03 input records without alteration or concatenating alternatives.
Tokenize each subject with the same case-folded words and Unicode symbol
features as CAP03, retaining their sequence. Compare three exhaustive pair
families, each requiring support from at least two distinct source notes:

1. Unordered: both distinct words occur anywhere in the same subject.
2. Ordered: the first word occurs before the second, with any gap.
3. Adjacent: the first word immediately precedes the second in the token sequence.

Repeated occurrences, subjects and aliases from one note do not create independent
support. Never form pairs across subject records. Punctuation is not a token;
hyphenated words remain comparable to space-separated words, as in CAP03.
Record token positions so punctuation boundaries can be audited rather than
mistaken for syntactic boundaries. Same-word pairs are excluded in every family.
There is no stopword removal, stemming, learned score, semantic selection,
closure expansion, or parameter sweep. Two-word patterns isolate the smallest
combination already implicated by CAP03; they are not proposed as a sufficient
vocabulary for the final system.

## Hypotheses and competing explanations

Order may remove the specific More-coffee/disconnection collision, while
adjacency further removes combinations formed across intervening clauses.
The stronger hypothesis is that adjacency can do this without dropping the
already demonstrated shared meanings. Counterevidence includes a faithful
common subject supported only through non-adjacent or reordered words.

Alternatively, stricter patterns may only trade accidental combinations for
missed relevant notes, leaving reporting-language candidates unchanged. Repeated
wording can also be supplied by the subject prompt instead of recurring topics.
Inspect source masks and originals to distinguish these explanations from a
mere reduction in candidate counts.

Use the earlier good-morning, processed-food, sea-salt, more-coffee and
rosemary/bread evidence as diagnostic witnesses, not an exhaustive expected
taxonomy. Inventory every adjacent pair; inspect potentially descriptive pairs
and their source evidence, accepting novel coherent meanings. Distinguish a
descriptor-only rejection (e.g. function/reporting words) from a semantic
judgment on the underlying notes. Preserve the full outputs, including errors.

Would either outcome change the next decision? Yes. If adjacency supplies useful
combinations while retaining dispersed examples, investigate its selection as a
candidate channel. If it loses those examples or leaves unrelated attachments,
do not adopt it as the sole representation: investigate structure that retains
relationships across gaps. If reporting phrases dominate, do not interpret
frequency or closed-pattern compression as semantic selection. A known phrase
being generated by exhaustive enumeration is an implementation check, not by
itself a research result. The consequential evidence is the observed tradeoff
on actual source meanings and memberships.

## Literature fit

AutoPhrase separates candidate n-grams from learned phrase quality; its model
uses Wikipedia-derived positives and corpus-statistical features. Its reported
corpora contain 91.6–808 million words. Those results do not establish that its
quality estimator will work on this small, repetitive set of generated subjects.
No AutoPhrase model is selected or trained here.[^1]

TopMine constructs contiguous phrases before topic modeling, combining adjacent
tokens according to corpus association. This motivates checking contiguity, not
transplanting a significance cutoff or equating its topic objective with our
navigation objective.[^2]

Béchet et al. explicitly distinguish gap constraints and closed patterns from
expert validation of useful linguistic patterns. Their application is French
qualifying expressions, with task-specific preprocessing—not semantic note
categories. We borrow the controlled gap distinction, not their complete method
or its empirical claims.[^3]

## Verification and preservation

Add tests of the actual construction function before implementation and observe
their expected failure. Verify ordered support is contained in unordered
support, and adjacent support in ordered support, for every pair and input row.
Verify token feature sets reproduce CAP03 and inputs remain byte-for-byte equal.
Run the production command twice to a new directory and compare results.
Preserve all baseline artifacts and keep the review tool unchanged.
